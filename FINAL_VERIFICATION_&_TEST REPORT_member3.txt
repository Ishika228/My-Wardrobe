4. Recognition Capabilities
The clothing classifier extracts contour aspect ratios, vertical/horizontal mass profiles, and structural shape markers to reliably recognize the following 14 clothing categories:

Target Category	Structural Geometric Markers Identified
Shirt	Collar/neck edge gradient, vertical button placket profile, medium aspect ratio
T-shirt	Crew/V-neck curvature, short-sleeve shoulder contour, absence of front placket
Jeans	Lower-body aspect ratio ($>1.35$), leg split contour, characteristic denim blue/indigo tone
Trousers	Lower-body aspect ratio ($>1.35$), leg split contour, neutral/dark tailored shades

Sneakers	Low horizontal aspect ratio ($AR < 0.85$), athletic sole contour, casual white/rubber base
Boots	Ankle-height aspect ratio ($0.85 \le AR \le 1.35$), boot shaft contour
Shorts	Lower-body silhouette with high leg split and short leg length ($AR < 1.0$)
Skirt	Lower-body single contour without leg split
Accessories	Compact bounding boxes and non-garment aspect profiles
5. Colour Detection Capabilities
Dominant and Secondary Color Extraction:
Segments foreground clothing pixels from background using filled polygon contour masks.
Applies K-Means clustering ($K=3$) in CIE LAB space (which matches human perceptual distance $\Delta E$).
Maps clustered centroids to the closest named shade in the 20-color fashion palette (

color_detector.py
).
Supported Palette: Black, White, Grey, Charcoal, Navy Blue, Blue, Beige, Brown, Tan, Olive Green, Emerald Green, Red, Burgundy, Pink, Yellow, Mustard, Orange, Purple, Lavender, Teal.
Lighting Invariance: Tested against high-key light garments (White linen) and low-key dark garments (Charcoal, Black, Navy).
6. Pattern Detection Capabilities
The pattern recognition engine combines frequency domain and spatial gradient analysis (

classifier.py
):

Plain: Low overall edge density ($E_{density} < 0.05$) and low spatial variance across image blocks.
Striped: Strong directional anisotropy where horizontal Sobel variance exceeds vertical variance ($V_y / V_x > 1.6$), accompanied by periodic 1D profile oscillation peaks ($Peaks_r \ge 10$).
Checked / Plaid: Bidirectional orthogonal Sobel variance ($V_x / V_y \in [0.45, 2.2]$) with simultaneous row and column periodic peaks ($Peaks_r \ge 12$ and $Peaks_c \ge 12$).
Floral: High organic edge density with high circular color variance across patches, lacking rigid grid peaks.
Printed / Graphic: Localized center patch variance significantly exceeding peripheral fabric variance ($V_{center} / V_{periphery} > 2.0$).

10. Known Limitations
Severely Wrinkled or Folded Garments:
The geometric classifier relies on silhouette aspect ratio and profile contours. If a pair of trousers is rolled into a ball or folded in half, its aspect ratio resembles a top or accessory. Photos should be laid flat or hung on a hanger.
Busy Background Interference:
Background subtraction operates optimally when clothing is photographed against neutral backgrounds (plain wall, wooden floor, bedsheet). High-contrast multi-colored backgrounds (e.g., patterned bedspreads) can introduce contour noise.
Micro-Prints vs Plain:
Very subtle, microscopic textures viewed at low resolution ($<100\times100$) may register as Plain rather than Printed. A minimum image size of $256\times256$ is recommended.
Complex Hybrid Items:
Hybrid garments like sleeveless trench-vests or skorts will map to the closest primary class (Jacket or Skirt).

const axios = require('axios');
const FormData = require('form-data');
const fs = require('fs');
async function analyzeUserClothingImage(imageFilePath) {
  const form = new FormData();
  form.append('file', fs.createReadStream(imageFilePath));
  const response = await axios.post('http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/ai/analyze-clothing', form, {
